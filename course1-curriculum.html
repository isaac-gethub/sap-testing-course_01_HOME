PREREQ

Controls Theory & Exam

Before you touch SAP at all, you learn the vocabulary and judgment framework every module after this one assumes you already have.

B0

Primary Goals of SAP Controls & Compliance

What You'll DoMap all seven reasons SAP controls exist — regulatory, fraud, financial integrity, operational, ITGC, accountability, stakeholder confidence — to their SAP activity and their non-SAP activity side by side.

On the JobYou’ll be able to answer “why does this control exist” for any finding you’re handed, in the specific language a CFO or audit committee expects, not just “because SOX says so.”

B1

Risk Types

What You'll DoClassify Financial, Compliance, and Operational risk, and apply the inherent-risk-versus-residual-risk framework to a real SoD conflict scenario.

On the JobYou’ll be able to correctly classify a risk in a Risk and Controls Matrix — the classification decision that determines whether your test procedure is substantive or regulatory.

B2

Control Types

What You'll DoDistinguish preventive from detective controls with concrete SAP examples, and apply the configured-versus-inherent-automated distinction to a real access scenario.

On the JobYou’ll be able to tell a client whether their control needs a configuration review or a “has this been disabled” check — two completely different test procedures that look identical from the outside.

B3

Control Nature

What You'll DoPlace a control on the descending scale from configured automated through IT-dependent manual to fully manual, and match each position to its required audit evidence.

On the JobYou’ll be able to correctly scope how much evidence a control actually needs — over-testing a fully automated control wastes budget, under-testing a manual one gets flagged by External Audit.

B4

Why Controls Matter

What You'll DoBuild the business case for a controls workstream in CFO-accessible language, and challenge the “lift-and-shift” instinct that carries old process design into a new S/4HANA system unchanged.

On the JobYou’ll be able to answer a program sponsor’s “why are we spending budget on this during implementation” question with the actual cost comparison, not a compliance platitude.

B5

Engagement Types

What You'll DoDescribe how QA, advisory, and full hands-on engagements differ in scope, deliverables, and stakeholder relationships.

On the JobYou’ll walk into your first engagement already knowing which of the three you’re in, instead of learning it by making the wrong assumption in week one.

B6

S/4HANA Lifecycle

What You'll DoMap the specific controls activities and deliverables across all four implementation phases — Solution Confirmation, Design and Build, Testing, and Cutover and Deploy.

On the JobYou’ll know exactly what a controls consultant is supposed to be producing in any phase of a live transformation project, not just what the finished state looks like.

B7

Reading FD Documents

What You'll DoApply a risk-first question sequence — business purpose, direction, frequency, error handling — to a real functional design document before extracting control parameters from it.

On the JobYou’ll be able to open a functional design document you’ve never seen and pull the control-relevant parameters out of it correctly on the first pass.

B8

Testing Phase

What You'll DoDefine sufficient evidence for config, transactional, and IT-dependent-manual controls — including why testing only the scenarios that should pass isn’t a real test.

On the JobYou’ll be able to defend your sample and your evidence standard to a reviewer or an external auditor without getting sent back to redo it.

B9

AI in Controls

What You'll DoIdentify exactly where AI agents add measurable value in a controls workstream — gap analysis, control proposal drafting, test coverage analysis — and where they don’t.

On the JobYou’ll be able to use AI tooling as genuine leverage in your own work instead of either ignoring it or trusting it somewhere it shouldn’t be trusted.

B10

Practitioner Capstone

What You'll DoWork through a full practitioner scenario that forces every prior module’s judgment call at once — consistency across teams, working with incomplete documentation, setting your own standard.

On the JobYou’ll have already made the mistakes this capstone puts in front of you once, in a training environment, instead of for the first time in front of a client.

Exam

Final Exam

What You'll DoAnswer 49 questions pooled from all eleven modules, each with a full explanation regardless of whether you got it right.

On the JobA passing score is a documented, dated record that you actually know the theory before you’re handed a live SAP session — the credential a hiring manager can point to, not just a course-completion badge.

Foundations

Optional, Before Book 01

For trainees with no prior hands-on SAP experience. Pure navigation — nothing here is a deficiency to investigate.

101

Finding Your Way Around: SAP GUI, Fiori, and SPRO

What You'll DoLog into both SAP GUI and Fiori Launchpad, and navigate SPRO’s configuration tree.

On the JobYou’ll never lose time in your first week just figuring out where things are — every later Book assumes this is already automatic.

102

Reviewing Users and Roles: SUIM, SU01, and PFCG in Display Mode

What You'll DoPull a user’s role assignments in SUIM, view their master record in SU01, and inspect a role’s authorization objects in PFCG without editing anything.

On the JobYou’ll be able to answer “what can this user actually do” from the system itself, not from what someone tells you their role is supposed to allow.

103

Reading Security and System Logs: SM20, SM19, and SM21

What You'll DoRead the Security Audit Log and System Log to reconstruct what actually happened in a session, and understand SM19’s logging configuration.

On the JobYou’ll be able to independently verify a claim about what a user did or didn’t do, instead of taking someone’s word for it.

104

Change Management and Transports: STMS, SE01, SCC4

What You'll DoView the transport queue in STMS, inspect an individual transport’s contents in SE01, and check client settings in SCC4.

On the JobYou’ll be able to trace any production change back to the transport that made it — the single most common evidence request in a change-management test.

105

Batch Jobs and Interfaces: SM37, WE02, BD87, and RFC Monitoring

What You'll DoMonitor scheduled background jobs in SM37 and check IDoc-based interface status in WE02 and BD87.

On the JobYou’ll be able to tell the difference between a job that failed and a job that silently did nothing — a distinction most reviews miss.

106

Displaying Financial Documents: FB03 and FBL3N

What You'll DoOpen a posted financial document in FB03 and pull a full population of general ledger line items in FBL3N.

On the JobYou’ll be able to independently re-pull any financial population yourself instead of relying on someone else’s extract.

107

GRC Access Risk Analysis and Table Evidence Pulls: GRAC_SPM and SE16N

What You'll DoRun a first GRC Access Risk Analysis and pull raw table data directly via SE16N.

On the JobYou’ll be able to bypass a report you don’t trust and go straight to the underlying table — the exact move every anchor Book’s completed exhibit relies on.

108

Database Basics and Evidence Discipline

What You'll DoLearn the evidence-capture convention used across the entire course — selection screen, then result, every time, labeled and dated.

On the JobYour workpapers will already match the documentation standard a reviewer expects, from your very first real test.

Part I

Access Control & Identity

Access Control/SoD is the domain most job postings ask for by name — this Part carries it in full, not as one topic among many.

1

The Access List ★

What You'll DoRun a GRC Access Risk Analysis at user level, then independently confirm the flagged conflict yourself through SUIM and PFCG.

On the JobYou’ll be able to find a segregation-of-duties conflict and prove it’s real — not just flag it and hand it to someone else to verify.

4

The Next Badge

What You'll DoTest new-hire and turnover access using SU01, SUIM, and the GRC Access Request History to confirm provisioning matched what was actually approved.

On the JobYou’ll be able to catch the specific joiner/mover/leaver gap that shows up in nearly every SOX access-control finding: access that outlived its approval.

7

The Fourth Chair

What You'll DoRun GRC Access Risk Analysis against a Procurement-specific ruleset and confirm via SE16N whether flagged access was ever actually used.

On the JobYou’ll be able to distinguish access that’s merely risky from access that’s actually been exploited — the difference between a finding and a false alarm.

13

The Tile

What You'll DoReview Fiori Launchpad catalog and role assignments and cross-check them against the backend authorization objects the tile actually calls.

On the JobYou’ll be able to test Fiori security as rigorously as classic GUI security, instead of treating a tile as a black box because it “just launches an app.”

16

The Firefighter Log ★

What You'll DoReview firefighter ID activation logs in GRAC_SPM and cross-check actual transactions executed against the System Log in SM20.

On the JobYou’ll be able to test emergency access the way an auditor actually wants it tested — not just “was it logged,” but “does the log match what was really done.”

17

The Leaver

What You'll DoConfirm a terminated user’s lock status in SU01, check for post-termination logins via SUIM and SM20, and verify their removal from access recertification.

On the JobYou’ll be able to close the exact gap that produces the most reputationally damaging access finding: a former employee who could still log in.

Part II

IT General Controls: Change & Operations

ITGC Change Management is the second domain nearly every posting names explicitly — this Part gives you all three years of the recurring thread plus the operations and code-security topics around it.

2

The Emergency Change ★

What You'll DoConfirm every transport’s contents and release timestamp in SE01, then cross-reference it against the change ticket system to catch anything logged as emergency but never justified.

On the JobYou’ll be able to test change management the way it actually breaks in practice — not “was there a ticket,” but “did the ticket exist before the change, not after.”

5

The Cutover

What You'll DoPull a full cutover-weekend transport queue across every affected system and cross-check patch-level changes via SPAM/SAINT.

On the JobYou’ll be able to test change management at the volume and speed of a real go-live weekend, not just a routine week.

8

The Third Party

What You'll DoConfirm a vendor’s transports appear in the standard SAP transport log and reconcile them against the vendor’s own ticketing export.

On the JobYou’ll be able to hold a third-party developer to the exact same change-control standard as an internal team — a gap most companies quietly don’t enforce.

18

The Untested Backup ★

What You'll DoReview backup job completion in DB13/DB12 and confirm whether a live restore test has ever actually been performed.

On the JobYou’ll be able to tell a client the difference between “we take backups” and “we’ve proven we can restore from them” — the gap that turns a routine control into a business-continuity crisis.

19

The Interface Gap ★

What You'll DoMonitor RFC calls in SM58, check IDoc processing status in WE02/WE05, and reprocess errored IDocs via BD87.

On the JobYou’ll be able to test system-to-system interfaces with the same rigor as a manual control — a scope area most junior testers skip because it feels “too technical.”

20

The Back Door

What You'll DoReview a custom transaction’s AUTHORITY-CHECK statements in SE38/SE80 and compare them against standard authorization defaults in SU24.

On the JobYou’ll be able to test custom ABAP code for a missing authorization check — the exact kind of gap that lets someone bypass every access control you just tested in Part I.

Part III

Record to Report

3

The Approval Limit ★

What You'll DoPull a population of journal entries via FBL3N and confirm each one’s approval workflow in SWI1/SWIA against the configured routing threshold.

On the JobYou’ll be able to prove a financial approval limit is actually enforced by the system, not just documented in a policy nobody checks.

6

The Other Ledger

What You'll DoTest an acquired entity’s controls whether or not they’ve migrated onto SAP yet, requesting the full population instead of a pre-selected sample.

On the JobYou’ll be able to walk into a post-acquisition environment and test controls that don’t yet run on your own systems — a scenario standard SAP training never covers.

9

The Tip Line

What You'll DoInvestigate one specific journal entry using FB03’s change-document history and independently re-pull the population via SE16N on BKPF/BSEG.

On the JobYou’ll be able to run a targeted investigation from a single tip or complaint, not just a routine sample-based test.

Part IV

SAP GRC Platform Modules

Testing whether the automated controls tools themselves are actually working — a level up from testing the controls they monitor.

14

The Rule That Never Fired ★

What You'll DoReview a GRC Process Control business rule’s exception history, confirm the underlying job actually ran via SM37, and independently query the source table to see what the rule should have found.

On the JobYou’ll be able to catch an automated control that’s configured correctly but silently never actually executing — invisible to anyone who only checks the configuration.

15

The Risk Register

What You'll DoReview the GRC Risk Management risk catalog and independently verify each risk’s linked mitigating control reference still exists and is active.

On the JobYou’ll be able to catch a risk register that looks complete on paper but points to controls that were quietly retired or never built.

Part V

Governance, Disclosure & Executive Reporting

10

The Signature Page

What You'll DoReview the issue/deficiency tracker’s discipline and your team’s status-reporting practice against what ultimately reaches a CFO’s signature.

On the JobYou’ll understand exactly how your day-to-day testing work turns into the sentence a CFO signs their name to.

11

The Comment Letter

What You'll DoAssemble evidence supporting an SEC response, organizing source documents chronologically and by control period.

On the JobYou’ll be able to produce an evidence package that survives direct SEC scrutiny, not just an internal review.

12

The Four-Day Clock ★

What You'll DoReconstruct a security-relevant event’s technical timeline using SM21/SM20 and cross-reference it against the transport log.

On the JobYou’ll be able to support a disclosure-controls review under a real regulatory deadline, with the technical evidence already assembled correctly.

Part VI

Legal & Compliance Adjacent Scope

21

The Retention Policy

What You'll DoIdentify records by age in HR and vendor master tables via SE16N and review Information Lifecycle Management retention rules where configured.

On the JobYou’ll be able to test a data-retention policy the way a privacy regulator actually checks it — against real record ages, not a written policy statement.

22

The Indirect Access

What You'll DoReview SAP’s own license measurement output in USMM and quantify indirect-access volume via table queries and workload analysis in ST03N.

On the JobYou’ll be able to flag a licensing exposure before it becomes an expensive true-up bill during a vendor audit.

Part VII

Business Application Controls

Matching, payments, tolerances, batch jobs, and asset parameters — the business-process control layer above ITGC that most competing courses don’t cover at all.

23

The Job That Stopped Running

What You'll DoReview a job’s actual execution history in SM37 with cancelled runs included, and cross-check it against SM36’s defined schedule.

On the JobYou’ll be able to catch a batch job that’s silently failing or silently doing nothing — invisible to a review that only checks for a “finished” status.

24

The Match That Wasn’t

What You'll DoPull purchase-order line items by plant via SE16N and confirm via ME23N and MB51 whether three-way match is actually enforced everywhere it’s supposed to be.

On the JobYou’ll be able to test a match control’s configuration itself, not just sample invoices against a control that may never have been capable of catching anything.

25

The File That Left Twice

What You'll DoCross-reference payment run tables directly via SE16N to confirm proposal creation and execution were performed by different people, and count transmission records per file.

On the JobYou’ll be able to catch a duplicate payment file transmission and the segregation-of-duties gap behind it, not just the payment run’s summary screen.

26

The Bank Detail Nobody Approved

What You'll DoBuild your test population from raw change-document tables rather than the workflow log, and calculate the day-count between a bank-detail change and the next payment.

On the JobYou’ll be able to test the single highest-value target in vendor-fraud schemes — a control most testers check from the wrong starting population.

27

The Tolerance That Swallowed It

What You'll DoPull invoice tolerance configuration via OMR6 and confirm whether an empty blocked-invoice list means the control works or means it’s set too wide to catch anything.

On the JobYou’ll be able to correctly read a “clean” control report as a possible red flag instead of automatically treating it as good news.

28

The Depreciation That Kept Running

What You'll DoGroup acquired assets by depreciation key via SE16N and confirm whether they were ever remapped to your own company’s depreciation policy.

On the JobYou’ll be able to catch a post-acquisition control gap that can run silently, without error, for years — successful execution and correct calculation are not the same assertion.

Part VIII

Order-to-Cash Controls

The revenue side of the business, held to the same standard as the money already covered in Part VII — credit release independence and billing cutoff.

29

The Held Order

What You'll DoPull the population of credit-blocked sales orders straight from table VBUK and compare each release against the order’s original creator in VA03.

On the JobYou’ll be able to spot a credit-hold control that’s become a formality the order’s own creator can clear alone, instead of assuming a block that eventually released was a block that worked.

30

The Early Invoice

What You'll DoReconstruct the billing population from tables VBRK/VBRP and compare each document’s creation timestamp against its delivery’s actual goods-issue time in VL03N.

On the JobYou’ll be able to catch revenue pulled forward to hit a quarter-end target — a violation a date-only comparison, instead of a timestamp comparison, would miss entirely.

Part IX

Treasury & Tax Controls

Bank reconciliation and tax configuration — exposure most junior testers never get assigned until someone asks them to look at it.

31

The Uncleared Item

What You'll DoPull the bank clearing account’s open-item aging directly via FAGLL03 and confirm every active house bank account is actually inside the reconciliation scope.

On the JobYou’ll be able to catch an account quietly dropped from a reconciliation report’s scope — a gap that looks like a clean result instead of the finding it actually is.

32

The Rate That Didn’t Update

What You'll DoCompare a tax code’s transport go-live date against its policy-effective date, then recompute the tax that should have posted using table BSET.

On the JobYou’ll be able to quantify exactly what a configuration change’s timing gap cost in real dollars, instead of noting the gap existed and moving on.

Part X

Adjacent Tools & Emerging Practice

The tools and methods increasingly sitting alongside native SAP GRC — third-party platforms, full-population data analytics, and AI-assisted review.

33

The Other Platform

What You'll DoRun the same SoD risk analysis on native SAP GRC AC and a third-party platform side by side, then reconcile any mismatch back to its ruleset.

On the JobYou’ll be able to work an engagement where SoD sign-off runs through Fastpath, Pathlock, or SailPoint instead of — or alongside — native SAP GRC, which a growing share of employers now expect.

34

The Spreadsheet That Found It

What You'll DoPull a full transaction population into a simple query and calculate every record’s variance against a control threshold in one pass, instead of a small manual sample.

On the JobYou’ll be able to recognize when a finding only exists in aggregate — and reach for SQL or Power BI instead of stretching a 25-item sample that was never going to find it.

35

The Recommendation Nobody Checked

What You'll DoCompare access-approval decisions against SAP GRC 2026’s Joule-generated recommendation and the elapsed decision time, to test whether real review is still happening.

On the JobYou’ll be able to speak fluently about AI-assisted GRC review in an interview — and know exactly what a rubber-stamped recommendation looks like in the data.

Part XI · Elective

Manager Track Elective — From Finding to Closure

Turn the findings from every other Part into a Risk & Control Matrix, a severity rating, and a defensible conclusion — the manager’s side of the engagement.

36

The Map Before the Testing

What You'll DoTurn a control-owner walkthrough into a fully attributed Risk & Control Matrix — owner, frequency, key/non-key, and testing approach — before any fieldwork begins.

On the JobYou’ll be able to build the document that drives an entire engagement’s testing plan, instead of just executing tests someone else scoped.

37

The Control That Was Never Built to Hold

What You'll DoReview a control’s role design in PFCG to determine whether it could ever enforce independence — independent of what any operating sample happened to show.

On the JobYou’ll be able to tell a design deficiency from an operating deficiency and send the right fix to the right owner, instead of re-testing a control that can’t pass no matter the sample size.

38

How Many Is Enough

What You'll DoApply a frequency-based sample-size standard across an engagement’s key controls, and retest whether earlier populations were sourced independently.

On the JobYou’ll be able to defend a sample size and a population source as two separate, equally necessary decisions, instead of treating a correctly-sized sample as automatically a correct one.

39

What Kind of Finding Is This

What You'll DoRate a set of engagement findings as deficiency, significant deficiency, or material weakness using one consistent framework — impact, pervasiveness, and compensating controls.

On the JobYou’ll be able to walk into an Audit Committee conversation with a severity call you can defend in one sentence, instead of a rating that was really just a gut feeling.
