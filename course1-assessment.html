Assessment & Format

The Full Sweep, one company, built the way a real engagement runs.

A live, instructor-led cohort — not a self-paced library. Two levels: Intermediate (19 Books, 16 sessions, 4 weeks) and Advanced (8 Books, 12 sessions, 3 weeks), each with a real instructor and the Live SAP window open every session. You're graded the way a reviewer grades a real engagement: three marked workpapers in Intermediate; an RCM, a deficiency memo and a final stakeholder panel in Advanced — and defending your conclusions at both levels.

How you're graded

SAP execution is the easy part. Ninety percent of your grade is judgement.

The same seven things a reviewer looks for on a real engagement — now the weighted rubric your three workpapers, your Defend It turns and your stakeholder panel are graded against. Every completed exhibit models them.

  • 20%Business risk & control understanding — can you say why this control exists before you test it
  • 15%Testing strategy & population definition — did you request the full population, or accept a pre-selected sample
  • 15%Evidence evaluation — does the evidence actually support the conclusion, or just look complete
  • 15%Consultant judgement & escalation — do you know when a finding needs to go up, not just get logged
  • 15%Workpaper & conclusion quality — would this survive a reviewer's first pass without a follow-up question
  • 10%Stakeholder communication — could you explain this finding to the SAP team and the audit team, in each one's own language
  • 10%SAP execution accuracy — the right transaction, the right screen, correctly documented

SAP execution is still checked every session through the evidence spot-checks — it feeds the 10% line. If you only ever get faster at clicking through transactions, that's the one line you'll improve, and the one that matters least once you're staffed on an engagement.

What's assessed

You don't just run the test. You defend it.

Every manager-level posting asks for the same thing junior training skips — standing behind a conclusion when an auditor, a developer or a CFO pushes back. These are the pieces you're graded on.

How it runs

Theory, then live system, every single session.

The order it runs in

  • Before session 1: Controls Theory prerequisite (B0–B10) and the 49-question exam, self-paced — plus Foundations (101–108) for anyone without prior hands-on SAP time
  • Intermediate, sessions 1–16 over 4 weeks: 15 Books live in order, 4 as guided self-study — each session Hour 1 Theory, Hour 2 live Demo Lab closing with 15 minutes of Defend It · Intermediate certificate
  • Advanced, sessions 1–12 over 3 weeks: the Manager Track (RCM workshop, design vs operating, sampling, deficiency classification), then third-party tools, analytics, AI-assisted review and Clean Core
  • Advanced session 12: stakeholder panel and close · Advanced certificate
  • Any time, during or after: the 19 Beyond the Core Books, self-paced

What's required between sessions

  • Independent repetition of that day's full transaction walkthrough on your own training-client login
  • The instructor spot-checks evidence at the start of the following class
  • Self-check against each Book's completed exhibit before your own evidence goes in the app
  • Build and submit each of the three workpapers by its due session — marked by a facilitator and returned within a week
