What's covered

Two levels. 27 core Books. One connected case.

Intermediate makes you job-ready for SOX/ITGC testing roles. Advanced adds what senior and lead postings ask for. Take Intermediate first, or go straight to Advanced after an entry check if you already test controls for a living.

Intermediate: Controls Testing — 16 sessions · 4 weeks · 19 Books
  • PART I — Access Control & Identity · 6 Books

    SoD conflicts, joiner/mover/leaver, procurement SoD, Fiori access and Firefighter logs.

  • PART II — Change & Operations · 4 Books

    Emergency change, custom-code authorization checks, backups and interfaces.

  • PARTS III–IV — Record to Report & GRC · 2 Books

    Journal-entry approval limits and the Process Control rule that never fired.

  • PART VII — Business Application Controls · 5 Books

    Batch jobs, three-way match, payment files, vendor bank changes and invoice tolerances.

  • PART VIII — Order-to-Cash · 2 Books

    Credit-block release independence and billing cutoff.

15 Books taught live, 4 as guided self-study checked in class · 3 marked workpapers.

Advanced: Manager Track & Emerging Practice — 12 sessions · 3 weeks · 8 Books
  • PART XI — Manager Track · 4 Books

    RCM construction, design vs operating effectiveness, sample size and deficiency classification — from finding to closure.

  • PART X — Adjacent Tools & AI · 3 Books

    Fastpath, Pathlock, SailPoint and AuditBoard, full-population analytics and Joule-assisted review.

  • PART XII — Clean Core / RISE · 1 Book

    Extensibility classification, transport governance and AI-generated code review.

  • Capstone

    RCM workshop, a deficiency memo, an Audit Committee readout and the stakeholder panel.

Entry: the Intermediate certificate, or an entry check for experienced testers.

Beyond the Core — 19 self-paced Books, yours to keep
  • Extensions to Parts II–IV and VII

    Cutover, third-party change, post-acquisition ledgers, tip-line investigations, risk registers and asset depreciation.

  • PART V — Governance & Disclosure

    How testing work becomes a CFO's signature and an SEC response.

  • PART VI — Legal & Compliance Adjacent

    Data retention and indirect-access licensing exposure.

  • PART IX — Treasury & Tax

    Bank reconciliation, tax configuration, FX hedge documentation and In-House Cash.

  • PART XIII — Payroll & Personnel

    Ghost employees, pay-rate changes, off-cycle payments and payroll-to-GL reconciliation.

Every single class

Every session is a lab. Not just some of them.

Every live session, at both levels, runs the same two-hour structure — no session is theory-only, no session is a passive watch.

Hour 1 — Theory

The scenario, the control objective, the pitfall.

The instructor walks that day's Book(s): the story, the control being tested, the real-world mistake it's built around, and what "good" evidence looks like — before anyone touches the system.

Hour 2 — Demo Lab

Live, on a real system, screen-shared.

The instructor runs the actual T-codes and Fiori apps for that day's topic on a live S/4HANA training client — the same screens you'll repeat yourself before the next class. The last 15 minutes is Defend It: one student presents the conclusion, the instructor pushes back as the reviewer.

Independent repetition is mandatory, not optional homework. Every student repeats that day's full transaction walkthrough on their own training-client login before the next session — the instructor spot-checks evidence at the start of the following class.
Judged like a real engagement

You don't just run the test. You defend it.

Clicking the right transaction is the easy part. Every manager-level posting asks for the harder part — standing behind a conclusion when an auditor, a developer or a CFO pushes back. So that's what you're graded on.

Every Demo Lab ends with Defend It.

One student presents that day's conclusion as if to an external auditor, and the instructor pushes back as the reviewer would. With five seats per class, you defend your work three times in Intermediate and twice more in Advanced — before you ever face it in an interview.

Intermediate · Three marked workpapers

Built and reviewed like real deliverables.

An access/SoD test after Part I, a change and operations test after Part II, and an ITAC test across Procure-to-Pay and Order-to-Cash — each marked by a facilitator and returned within a week.

Advanced · Your own RCM

You map it before you test it.

You build the Thornfield Risk & Control Matrix from your Intermediate testing, then write a deficiency memo — the documents a manager uses to scope and close an engagement.

Advanced · Final stakeholder panel

One finding. Three audiences.

In the last Advanced session you present a real finding three times — to an auditor, an SAP developer and a CFO — each in their own language.
